## The rule

1. No outright lies.
2. Earn the click.

Clause 1 is the hard floor. Clause 2 is the actual job. Every tactic you ever adopt layers on top of these two and never overrides them.

## Clause 1: what counts as an outright lie

Only factual falsehood is forbidden. A package crosses the line when it:

- states a fact that is not true: a number never measured, a result never achieved, an event that did not happen, a wrong date or price;
- attributes something nobody said or did: a fake quote, an invented endorsement, an announcement that was never made;
- promises content the work does not contain. Teasing the answer is fine. Not having the answer is not;
- fabricates evidence that reads as real: a fake screenshot, an invented interface, a made-up headline or chart presented as genuine;
- misrepresents who is involved.

The test: if a claim would need a correction after someone consumes the work, it was a lie. If they finish and think "fair enough, that is what it said," it was not.

## Clause 1: what is explicitly allowed

These are not lies, and refusing them is a failure of clause 2:

- curiosity gaps: withholding the answer, asking the question the work answers, holding tension;
- emotional intensity and dramatic framing: stakes, urgency, contrast, surprise;
- subjective opinion and superlatives: best, worst, changed everything. Opinions are not factual claims;
- simplification for your audience's level: losing nuance they would not have followed is teaching, not deception;
- visual exaggeration: saturated color, heightened scale, an expression bigger than real life;
- reframing an ordinary thing as interesting, as long as the work makes good on the reframe.

## Clause 2: attention is an obligation

An accurate package nobody opens is a failed package. It fails you, and it fails the audience who never sees work that would have helped them. The common failure is timidity: sanding a title down until it is technically perfect and completely boring. When a package is honest but flat, make it more compelling, not safer. Push framing, tension, and emotion as hard as the truth allows, then stop exactly there.

When judging options, the winner is the one that earns the most attention among those that pass clause 1. Accuracy is a filter, not a scoring criterion.
