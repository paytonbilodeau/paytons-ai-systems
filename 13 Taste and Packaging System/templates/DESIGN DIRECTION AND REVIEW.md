# Design Direction and Review

Use this for substantial visual work. Keep approved brands, existing visual styles, and the user's scope. A small correction needs a small correction, not a new design exploration.

## Define the task

- Who is the design for, and where will they encounter it?
- What should they understand or do first?
- Which facts, content, dimensions, brand rules, and accessibility needs are fixed?
- What feeling should the design support, and which choices may change?
- What artifact and evidence will demonstrate completion?

## Explore three useful directions

Vary composition, information order, interaction, or visual metaphor. Name the benefit and cost of each. Different colors on the same layout do not constitute meaningful exploration. When a style is locked, change the scene or structure inside it.

If ideas converge, an optional recorded random seed can suggest new combinations. Keep facts and locked constraints outside random selection. A seed can reproduce a starting brief; it does not guarantee quality or identical model output. Reject combinations that do not serve the task.

Study a few relevant references. Record their source, viewing context, the mechanism worth learning, and what not to copy. Similar task and information density matter more than a famous brand name.

## Build and review separately

Build the primary experience and relevant alternate states. Keep interface text and controls semantic. Use generated artwork or motion only when it has a clear role, and check the available tools and costs before generation. Provide static or reduced-motion alternatives where appropriate. Preserve official logo structure while matching its rendering to the surrounding image's style.

Separate visual craft from functional checks. For visual critique, provide the brief, locked constraints, screenshots, and comparable references. If fresh-context delegation is authorized and available, exclude code, builder identity, earlier scores, and implementation history from that packet. Otherwise label the review as self-review. Either kind of model review can be biased.

Ask for observed gaps, their user consequences, three prioritized improvements, and one element to preserve. For close comparisons, anonymize candidates and reverse their order. Allow ties and uncertainty. A taste score cannot compensate for a false claim, unreadable content, wrong logo, or broken control.

## Refine without chasing a score

Start with at most two visual refinement rounds. Change the few things with the largest benefit, capture the result, and keep or revert based on evidence. Continue only for a concrete unresolved problem or a requested further exploration. Mandatory correctness checks still need completion.

Remove choices that supply no information, grouping, affordance, feedback, or useful identity. Keep necessary labels and explanation. Cards, gradients, familiar layouts, and particular fonts are not inherently wrong or proof of AI authorship. Repair the observed defect rather than replacing one visual formula with another.

Edit copy for truthful claims, concrete actions, and the user's voice. Do not describe AI-edited text as human-written.

## Record evidence

For interfaces, record the main task, alternate states, keyboard behavior, focus, viewport/reflow checks, readable text, measured contrast, motion preferences, and errors. For images, record full-size and small-display review, crop/dimensions, exact words, brand structure, and stylistic integration. Automated checks do not certify complete accessibility or real-user success.

Save the artifact, chosen direction, review context, changes, tests actually completed, and unresolved limits in the taste ledger. A local demo is not a deployed service. One result does not establish a universal preference or change model weights.
