## Layer 1: purpose

- Does the piece recommend, decide, or claim something, or does it only explain?
- Would it read the same if a competitor had written it about their own work?
- What was deliberately left out? A piece that covers every angle chose none.
- Does the causal path connect the evidence to the conclusion, or does fluent language hide a jump?

**Finding:** [pass, or what is missing]

## Layer 2: voice

- Does one person with one point of view seem to have written it?
- Is anything hedged that the writer actually believes flatly?
- Does the temperature vary, or is every sentence equally enthusiastic?

**Finding:** [pass, or where the voice collapses]

## Layer 3: padding

- Cut the first paragraph. Did anything break?
- Find each sentence that restates the previous one in different words.
- Find filler depth: lists that exist to look thorough rather than to be used.

**Finding:** [pass, or what to cut]

## Layer 4: surface patterns

Flag and fix the machine tells, in the writer's own vocabulary rather than a thesaurus's:

- binary contrast constructions: "It's not X. It's Y."
- throat-clearing openers and faux-insight setups: "Here's the thing," "What nobody tells you";
- inflated transitions: "Moreover," "Furthermore," "In today's fast-paced world";
- empty intensity: "game-changing," "the possibilities are endless";
- symmetrical triads everywhere, rhetorical questions as paragraph starters, and a summary that re-announces what was just said.
