# Sketch Starter Card

This example is based on Payton's Sketch system.

## Definition

A rough-but-coherent explainer sketch with dry charcoal or blue-gray pencil lines, a mostly white background, one strong accent family, simple acting, and one visual thesis that reads before the words do. It should look visibly drawn and corrected, not digitally inked and polished.

## Essential rules

- Reduce the idea to one scene.
- Use a clear line hierarchy: strongest on the focal subject, lighter on supporting props, and palest on sparse background suggestions.
- Leave roughly 5 to 15 percent of pale construction and correction marks visible.
- Use occasional doubled contours, small overshoots, open joins, uneven pressure, and slightly wobbly arcs.
- Keep silhouettes and important props coherent even when the individual strokes are imperfect.
- Draw compact, slightly awkward anatomy and simple dot-and-line faces instead of polished facial planes or heroic proportions.
- Apply accent color as a light, loose flat wash with tiny gaps or slight drift at some edges. Do not clip every fill perfectly.
- Keep most of the image neutral and use one accent color for focus.
- Use sparse hand-lettered text only when the scene needs it.
- Prefer a storyboard frame over an infographic layout.
- Keep the core subject readable after common social crops.
- When a person recurs, lock a short character description and silhouette across every prompt.
- Turn each new topic into an original metaphor. References teach mechanisms, not scenes to recreate.

## Avoid

- polished vector art;
- thick, smooth finished ink;
- polished faces, anatomy, or hair that feel designed instead of sketched;
- scratchy residue across the whole frame that obscures the silhouette;
- perfectly clipped glossy fills;
- realistic lighting;
- crowded dashboards;
- balanced rainbow palettes;
- long generated text;
- a generic mascot pose with no action.

## Test

Create three unrelated concepts and render one of them as both a wide video frame and a vertical social frame. The visible underdrawing, corrected strokes, line hierarchy, character silhouette, negative space, accent rule, and two-second read should remain stable without reusing the same composition. Reject any result that turns the pencil work into smooth finished ink.
