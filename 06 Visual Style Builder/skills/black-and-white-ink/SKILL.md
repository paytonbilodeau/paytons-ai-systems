# Black-and-White Ink

Generate a conceptual black ink drawing on a pure white background. The style's power is restraint: one symbolic action, confident lines, and white space doing half the work.

## Before generating

Use the current runtime's native image generation through the user's existing
subscription by default. A paid API fallback needs explicit approval, current
operation-specific documentation and an attempt budget. Missing tools or
required references do not authorize spending or dropping the references.

1. Reduce the idea to one symbolic action or one contrast. If the concept needs a caption to make sense, the concept is not ready.
2. Decide the focal subject and what merely supports it. Line weight will carry that hierarchy.
3. Choose close-up or wide framing deliberately; both must feel like the same ink language.

## Prompt structure

Scene first, then style law, in this order.

Style law to include every time: black ink illustration on a pure flat white background; confident hand-drawn linework; varied line weight separating the focal subject from supporting detail; selective hatching only where it clarifies form or depth; generous intentional white space; bold composition that reads small; main idea centered inside a safe crop area.

## Hard rules

- Black ink and white background only.
- Line weight is the hierarchy tool: heaviest on the focal subject, lighter on support.
- Hatching earns its place by clarifying form; decorative hatching is noise.
- One symbolic action or contrast per image.
- Keep any text to a few words at most, and only when the user asked for text.

## Never

- color, gray washes, or gradients;
- paper grain, notebook texture, or aged-paper effects;
- borders and frames;
- decorative detail with no role;
- long text or labeled-diagram clutter;
- crowding the edges of the frame.

## QA before delivering

Check at full size and small: the background is truly white, the focal subject wins at a glance, the drawing still reads at avatar size, and nothing decorative snuck in. Test the style's consistency by imagining the last image in the set beside this one; they should feel drawn by the same hand.
