# Deadpan Reality Clash

Generate an image where flat two-dimensional cartoon people sit inside a detailed realistic environment. The comedy and the clarity both come from the deliberate visual separation and the restraint of the expressions.

## Before generating

1. Choose one scene that reads in a glance. The situation carries the idea; the style carries the tone.
2. Decide the emotion, then dial it down one notch. Deadpan means grounded expressions and restrained body language, not theatrical reactions.
3. Pick the realistic setting for what its materials say: an office's glass and carpet, a kitchen's steel and steam. The background does the world-building.

## Prompt structure

Scene first, then the two-layer law, in this order.

Two-layer law to include every time: foreground people are flat two-dimensional cartoons with solid fill colors and thin uniform black outlines, no shading, no gradients, no texture, no highlights, no cast shadows; the environment is detailed, photorealistic, and materially specific; the two layers remain visibly separate, with no global filter unifying them.

## Hard rules

- Cartoon figures stay perfectly flat: solid colors, uniform thin outlines, nothing borrowed from the background's light.
- The background stays convincingly real and materially specific.
- The separation is the style. Anything that blends the layers, rim light on the cartoons, painterly texture on the foreground, a unifying color grade, breaks it.
- Expressions match the idea with restraint; a raised eyebrow beats a scream.
- Text only when asked, short, and never explaining the whole concept.

## Never

- three-dimensional or shaded cartoon rendering;
- exaggerated cartoon-reaction faces;
- painterly or textured foreground figures;
- global filters, vignettes, or grades that unify both layers;
- cast shadows from cartoon figures onto the real scene;
- a caption that does the joke's work for it.

## QA before delivering

Check at full size and small: the cartoons are genuinely flat, the background could pass as a photograph on its own, the two layers stay separate everywhere they touch, and the character system would survive being placed into a completely different setting unchanged.
